Czech towns, hospitals and regions can wait months to sign a contract when a losing bidder asks the competition office to review the tender [S1,S2].

- Bidders asked for 268 reviews in 2025, up from 195 in 2024 [S1].
- A contract under review was blocked for 5 months on average [S2].
- The office ordered 141 interim measures in 2025, such as bans on signing [S1,S4].

The competition office reviews public tenders when a bidder complains, and its decisions can be appealed [S1].

- With an appeal, the wait grows to 7 to 9 months, and 45% of disputes were appealed in 2022 [S2].
- Tenders under first review in 2022 were worth about 80bn CZK [S3].
- The ministry puts the cost of each extra day of waiting at about 6M CZK of public money [S2].
- The office's head says many challenges come from badly prepared tenders and over-complicated conditions that invite bidders to attack them [S1].
- The office says the buyer alone answers for lawful conditions, and any fault in them counts against the buyer [S1].
- The office says it steps into at most 2–3% of tenders, mostly large ones [S1].
- Bidders lodged 205M CZK in deposits to file challenges in 2025, and 70M CZK of it went to the state [S1].

Existing non-solutions: Established Czech tender administrators and law firms already write tender conditions for public buyers and defend them, by hand [S10].

- Firms registered as far back as 1998 run tenders for towns and hospitals [S10].
- Law firms represent towns before the competition office, case by case [S10].
- A free state AI check covers only small contracts the office never reviews [S10].

No Czech product was found that checks a tender's conditions against the office's past decisions before it is published [S10]. Young AI tools answer questions about those decisions or draft documents from earlier tenders [S10]. The rows are under [Market gap](#competition).

Why now: No new deadline forces buyers to act, but challenges rose by a third in 2025, and each one can freeze a contract for months [S1,S2].

- A hospital company was barred from signing a 24M CZK contract in May 2026 [S4].
- Each extra day of review costs public budgets about 6M CZK [S2].
- Court review after the office took about 550 days on average [S2].

The dates behind this:

- In June 2024 the regional development ministry drafted a bill to speed up reviews, and it was withdrawn before it passed [S3].
- The review numbers rose from 195 in 2024 to 268 in 2025 [S1].

Who pays: Yes, towns already pay law firms and tender administrators to write tender documents and to defend tenders at the office [S10].

- Towns pay law firms monthly for work on one tender's documents [S10].
- Towns sign one-off contracts to be represented in a single review [S10].
- Small firms publish fixed prices per tender and per objection [S10].

What one town paid to defend a tender, and another for its tender documents, is under [Willing to pay](#willing-to-pay).

Solved elsewhere: In Germany and the United States, established firms prepare and check public buyers' tender documents, by hand and in software [S8,S9].

In Germany, a publisher has helped public buyers run tenders for over 30 years and counts 634 public buying offices as users [S8]. In the United States, software that builds tender documents to each agency's own rules serves about 1,900 agencies and cities [S9]. See [Validated abroad](#validated-abroad).

## First moves

1. Build a list of the tender conditions the competition office most often strikes down, from its own published decisions. The office's head names over-complicated conditions as a common cause, as [The opportunity](#opportunity) shows, so start with the conditions his office keeps overturning.
2. Ask the procurement heads of a few hospitals and mid-sized towns which of their tenders were challenged lately, and how long each contract waited. Their answers tell you which kinds of tenders to check first and what a month of delay means to them.
3. Offer a fixed-price second look at a tender's conditions before it is published. Sell it to buyers who write their own tenders, and to the firms under [Market gap](#competition) as a second pair of eyes on their work.
4. Follow every new review request and signing ban the office publishes, and tell the affected buyer what similar cases decided. A buyer already caught in a review needs its defence fast, as [Why now](#why-now) shows.
