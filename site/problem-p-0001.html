The problem

Czech law has allowed electricity sharing between buildings since August 2024 (Lex OZE II, via the national EDC data hub). Two years in, 49,388 connection points and 1,136 apartment buildings across 218 sharing groups participate, and 137 GWh have been shared — June 2026 alone was a record month at over 21 GWh, 3× year on year. The official registry counts 133 energy communities, up from near zero in 2024.

Yet communities run member administration, allocation keys and settlement by hand. Because groups over 50 members must use fixed allocation keys, industry reporting says communities often lose up to half of the electricity they generate as unredistributable surplus. The state hub exchanges data between market participants; it explicitly does not do community administration, billing, or optimization. Nobody in Czechia sells software that does. Austria, running the same framework since 2021, counts 3,000+ renewable-energy communities — 23× Czechia.
