## 1. The brand's soul

**Core idea in one sentence:**
*The scarcest asset of the AI age is a well-stated problem — we publish them, city by city, with the money and the proof attached, so the person whose life uniquely fits each one can claim it.*

**Manifesto:**

> Somewhere between your flat and your morning coffee, you pass six problems nobody has claimed. The queue that shouldn't exist. The form that still wants a stamp. The empty ground floor on the square. You've noticed them for years. Noticing is the itch.
>
> Here's what changed: the solving got easy. The machines are good now. What's scarce isn't the ability to build — it's a problem stated well enough to be worth building against. So we state them. City by city, with the evidence, the money already on the table, and proof that someone in Rotterdam or Vilnius already cracked the same nut.
>
> You are not waiting for an idea. You are standing on one. You know the ground, the people, the shortcuts — the Czech way, the Bavarian way, the way things around here actually get done. That isn't local knowledge. That's home-field advantage.
>
> Find your problem. Say it's yours. Go.

**What we call the user: SOLVERS.** The platform's verb: **claim**. Why: it's the AI-era's own word, and in Czech the person legally responsible for a grant project is literally titled *"řešitel" — solver* — the identity is bureaucratically pre-installed in the home market; we just give the title swagger. ("Fixers" is mercenary, "locals" is passive, "opportunity hunters" is LinkedIn slop.)

## 2. Voice

Evidence-first, never breathless. Speaks in proper nouns — streets, sums, deadlines. Dry Central-European understatement (Cimrman-adjacent). Respectfully provocative — challenges, not cheerleading. Civic pride, zero folklore.

- **Tweet:** "Ostrava spends CZK 40M a year towing abandoned cars. Ghent halved it with an €80k web app. The tender closes in June. Somebody take this."
- **Card headline:** "Brno — 4,000 parking complaints go unanswered every year. CZK 12M tender open. Solved in Aarhus."
- **404 page:** "Nothing here. The problem you were looking for was claimed, solved, or never properly stated. Unlike your city, this page has no open problems."
- **Empty state:** "No open problems match your filters. Either paradise, or bad filters."
- **Anti-example (never):** "A MASSIVE untapped opportunity is waiting for YOU!"

## 3. The Brno / Munich test — top names shown, not described

**HOMEFIELD** *(the recommended direction)*

> **Brno has 214 open problems. Three of them have your name on them.**
> Real problems, stated properly — with the tender money attached and proof they've been solved abroad. You know this ground better than anyone who could beat you to it. That's the advantage. Claim one.

> *(Munich, expansion day one)* **München hat 214 offene Probleme. Drei davon gehören dir.** You know which Amt to call and which café the decision actually happens in. That's home-field advantage. Use it.

Why it wins: it's the only name that names the *user's advantage* rather than our database — the founder's vision ("this exactly fits who I am and my network") compressed into one word every European understands through football (Heimvorteil / domácí výhoda). It scales city-by-city with zero translation.

**MEZERA** *(runner-up — cleanest collision profile)*

> **Every city is full of gaps. We found yours.**
> Mezera is the evidence-backed feed of local problems: the complaint data, the funding already on the table, the city abroad that already solved it. *Mezera* is Czech for "the gap." It's also the space bar. Press it.

Wears its Czech birthplace as a badge the way Škoda or Miro do. Loses to Homefield only because it names the market's gap, not the solver's identity.

**UNSOLVED** *(the bold one)*

> **Brno, unsolved.**
> A live registry of your city's open problems — properly stated, publicly funded, proven solvable elsewhere. Status: open. Assignee: nobody, yet.

Caveat: culturally shadowed by true-crime ("Unsolved Mysteries") — usable only with total design confidence.

## 5. Visual identity brief (for a designer)

- **Logo:** lowercase grotesque wordmark; the "o" doubles as a **status dot** — hollow = open, filled = claimed. The GitHub-issue dot made civic.
- **Signature device: the claim.** A chalk/cadastral outline drawn around a fragment of real map + a rubber-stamp overlay ("CLAIMED — 14. 6. 2026 — @jana_brno") that fires when a solver takes a card. Cards read as official parcels of opportunity: gazette × land registry × issue tracker.
- **Color:** paper white + ink black; one **rubber-stamp red** (úřední razítko) for evidence and urgency; one signal green for open/claimed/solved. No SaaS gradient-purple, ever.
- **Type:** contemporary grotesque from a Czech foundry (Displaay / Superior Type territory) — full diacritics (ř, ě, ü, ß) non-negotiable and quietly on-brand. All money figures, tender numbers and deadlines in **tabular monospace** — data always looks like evidence, never marketing.

## 6. Naming traps (why some obvious options die)

Idea-slop names (IdeaHub, Sparkboard) — the moat is evidence vs. ideas; the name must never smell of brainstorm confetti. NGO/EU-project aesthetic (Civic-, -4Good, acronyms) repels commercial builders. Descriptive genericness (GapMap, OppRadar) is unownable consultancy-tool energy. False friends kill: *Prospekt* = advertising brochure in both Czech and German; *ř* dies in Munich. And note: **"díra" is also Czech slang for a dump of a town** — "Díra na trhu" was a good joke and a bad identity; it low-key insults the very cities we're romanticizing. Retire it to a newsletter section title at most. Finally, the grievance trap: leading with complaints reads as a city-hall portal — the brand must tilt toward *claim and advantage*, not lament.
