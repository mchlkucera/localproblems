Czech towns, firms and charities can be ordered to pay back part of a grant when a tender or report breaks its rules [S1].

- Tax offices ordered 977 paybacks worth 365.3M CZK in 2025 [S2].
- Tenders run against the procurement law are the most common fault [S1].
- Tax offices also often found false or distorted counts in grant reports [S1].

The payback is called a levy for breaching budget discipline. It is a tax-office order to return grant money that was spent against the grant's conditions [S4].

- Tax offices audited 1,816 grants worth about 8bn CZK in 2025 [S1].
- Those audits covered 366 municipalities, 495 companies and 858 other bodies such as charities, plus 89 state offices [S1].
- On top of the 365.3M CZK in paybacks, grant holders were charged 273.8M CZK in late penalties [S1].
- The share of audited money ordered back rose from 5.69% in 2023 to 7.90% in 2025 [S1].
- Some grants pay a fixed amount per unit delivered, such as a child cared for or a trainee trained, instead of per receipt [S1].
- In one such case, attendance sheets did not match who attended, and units were claimed for staff on leave or off sick [S1].
- In another, a grant holder set a tender's value too low, so it used the wrong, lighter procedure [S1].
- Other common faults were missed deadlines, missed targets and follow-up reports never filed, sometimes years after the project [S1].
- Small towns have the least staff and software to check their own spending, the finance ministry says [S1].
- Research grants are one part of this: auditors found 8 of 14 sampled farm-research projects broke their grant contract, with suspected paybacks in 4 [S3].
- The state auditor found 7 of 19 sampled brownfield-regeneration projects broke the tender law or the grant's spending rules, with suspected paybacks of up to 32.4M CZK [S11].
- In one grant-funded social-care project, a single contract change made without a new tender put up to 14.8M CZK of a 15.8M CZK grant at risk [S12].

Existing non-solutions: Established Czech grant consultants and tender administrators already run and check grant tenders and reports by hand, and towns pay them [S10].

- Firms registered as far back as 1998 sell towns tender and grant paperwork [S10].
- One firm promises childcare operators keep their grant even after an inspection [S10].
- Software for towns tracks grant deadlines and files, but checks no rules [S10].

No Czech product was found that reads a tender file or a grant report and checks it against the call's own rules before it is filed [S10]. The finance ministry trained about 4,050 public staff for free in 2025, including staff of small towns in their own regions [S1]. The rows are under [Market gap](#competition).

Why now: No new deadline forces grant holders to act, but paybacks land every year, and each one comes with a late penalty [S1,S4].

- A payback averaged about 374,000 CZK in 2025, before any penalty [S2].
- Grant holders asked to have 395.6M CZK waived, and almost 118M CZK was [S2].
- 98 appeals and 35 lawsuits were filed against these orders in 2025 [S1,S2].

The dates behind this:

- The payback rule has stood since 2000 [S4].
- On 27 November 2025 the EU Court of Justice settled how long tax offices have to act on EU-funded grants, and the tax offices changed their practice to match [S2].
- Tax audits of EU-funded grants had fallen 46% in 2025 while that question was open [S1].
- A new law on managing and checking public money replaces the current one on 1 January 2027 [S1].
- 22 of the gravest cases, such as suppliers picked in advance, were reported as crimes in 2025 [S2].

Who pays: Yes, towns already pay outside firms to run grant tenders and grant paperwork, per tender and per month [S10].

- 1,769 payment orders charged grant holders 639.1M CZK in 2025 [S1].
- 193 grant holders' requests sought waivers of levies and penalties in 2025 [S2].
- Towns list these consultant fees in their own published invoice books [S10].

What towns paid for a tender, a nursery's monthly grant paperwork and a follow-up report is under [Willing to pay](#willing-to-pay).

Solved elsewhere: In the United States and Poland, established firms help grant holders keep their spending and reports within the rules [S8,S9].

In the United States, software tracks a grant's compliance, reporting and money for the governments that give and receive grants, with over 220 customers [S8]. In Poland, a consultancy has run and settled EU-funded projects for towns and firms since 2004, for 342 clients [S9]. See [Validated abroad](#validated-abroad).

## First moves

1. Build a checklist for one per-unit grant, such as childcare, that compares attendance records with the units claimed before each report goes out. That is the kind of fault the tax offices name, as [The opportunity](#opportunity) shows. Run it for one operator for a quarter and show where the two did not match.
2. Ask the finance heads of a few small towns which grant tenders an outside firm runs for them and which they run themselves. The finance ministry says small towns have the least staff to check their own spending, so the ones they run alone are where a second look helps; see [The opportunity](#opportunity).
3. Offer a fixed-price second look at a finished tender file before the town signs the contract. Sell it to towns that run their own tenders, and to the firms under [Market gap](#competition) as a second pair of eyes on their work.
4. Collect the tax offices' and the courts' published decisions on these paybacks and turn them into the list of faults you check. Grant holders fight many of these orders, as [Why now](#why-now) shows, so the decisions spell out what was rejected and why.
