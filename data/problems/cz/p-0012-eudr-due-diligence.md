The EUDR — the EU's deforestation law, Regulation 2023/1115 — reaches Czech firms trading cattle, cocoa, coffee, palm oil, rubber, soy and wood: furniture and wood processors, coffee roasters, food producers, tyre and rubber importers [S1]. From 30 December 2026 each must trace its goods to the plots they came from and file a DDS (a due diligence statement carrying those coordinates), micro and small firms from 30 June 2027 [S1]. Penalties reach 4% of EU turnover [S1].

Why now: Parliament voted the second postponement on 11 December 2025, and the simplifications settle the obligation's final shape: only the first firm placing goods on the EU market files the statement, and micro and small primary operators get a one-off simplified declaration [S2].

Who pays: importers and processors in the wood and furniture, coffee, food and rubber chains, who need plot coordinates collected, supplier questionnaires chased and the statement filed into their customs paperwork [S1].

Existing non-solutions: the Czech field is unchecked — no local vendor is named, none ruled out. EU-wide compliance software sells into the obligation, but mid-sized Czech importers are not who those vendors' sales teams call. The nearest local candidates are the celní deklaranti — customs declarants, who already file these firms' import paperwork — and the software houses behind them; whether any bundles the filing is untested.
